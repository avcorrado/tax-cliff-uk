UK income tax and benefits, tax year 2026/27

The £100,000 cliff

Between £100,000 and £125,140 every extra pound you earn is taxed at an effective 62% in England. For parents, earning a single pound over the line can switch off thousands of pounds of childcare help. Answer a few quick questions and see what it does to you.

  • 62% effective rate on each extra pound between £100,000 and £125,140 in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (69.5% in Scotland, 71% with a Plan 2 loan).
  • £15,405 of childcare support lost in a single pound for a household with a toddler and a preschooler in full time nursery.
  • £125,140 is where the Personal Allowance finally reaches zero. Above it the rate falls to 47%, lower than the rate you paid on the way up.
  • 2010 is when the taper began. The £100,000 threshold has never been raised, and allowances are frozen until April 2031.

        How the cliff actually works

        There is no single £100,000 rule. Several separate rules stack up on the same income range, which is why the effect is so severe. Each one is modelled in the explorer.

        Normal rates Child Benefit charge Charge in full 62% zone 47% Childcare cliff
        £0£60,000£80,000£100,000£125,140£150,000

        1 The Personal Allowance taper

        Everyone gets the first £12,570 of income tax free. For every £2 of adjusted net income above £100,000 you lose £1 of that allowance, and by £125,140 it has gone completely.

        The maths for each extra £1 in England

        40p tax on the pound itself

        20p tax on the 50p of allowance you lose

        2p National Insurance

        62p gone, 38p kept

        In Scotland the advanced rate is 45%, so the same maths gives 67.5% before National Insurance.

        2 Child Benefit clawback

        The High Income Child Benefit Charge takes back 1% of your Child Benefit for every £200 of the higher earner's income above £60,000, and all of it at £80,000. In 2026/27 Child Benefit is £27.05 a week for the first child and £17.90 for each other child.

        That adds roughly 7 points of extra tax for one child and 12 for two on the way up. It is tested on the higher earner, not the household, so two people on £55,000 never pay it.

        3 Tax Free Childcare, a true cliff

        The government tops up what you pay a registered childcare provider by 20p for every 80p, worth up to £2,000 per child a year. Both parents need adjusted net income of £100,000 or less.

        At £100,001 it is all gone. There is no taper, no grace and no partial credit. One pound of extra income can cost a family thousands.

        4 30 hours funded childcare

        Working parents in England get 30 funded hours a week for 38 weeks for children from 9 months to school age. The same £100,000 test applies to each parent. Three and four year olds still keep the universal 15 hours.

        Scotland funds hours for 3 and 4 year olds without an income test, which the explorer reflects. Wales and Northern Ireland run different schemes that are not modelled.

        5 Student loans

        Plan 1, 2, 4 and 5 loans take 9% of pay above a threshold, and a Postgraduate loan takes 6%. The charge is based on pay, not adjusted net income, so a personal pension does not reduce it but salary sacrifice does.

        A Plan 2 graduate inside the taper pays 40% + 20% + 2% + 9%, an effective 71%.

        6 National Insurance

        Employees pay 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above. National Insurance does not cliff at £100,000 but it adds 2 points to the effective rate.

        Salary sacrifice pensions also save National Insurance. From April 2029 that relief is due to be capped at £2,000 a year, which the explorer does not model.

        7 The rate falls after the cliff

        Once the allowance is fully withdrawn at £125,140, the marginal rate drops back to 45% plus 2% National Insurance. A person on £110,000 pays more tax on their next pound than a person on £500,000.

        The pattern is a hump, not a ladder, and it is a side effect of how the allowance is withdrawn rather than a deliberate rate.

        8 Frozen thresholds

        The £100,000 threshold has not changed since the taper began in 2010. The Personal Allowance, higher rate threshold and additional rate threshold are frozen until April 2031. Pay rises keep pulling more people towards the cliff while the line stays put.

        Take home pay for you and any partner, after income tax, National Insurance, student loan and pension or Gift Aid payments, plus Child Benefit, minus the High Income Child Benefit Charge, minus what you really pay for childcare after funded hours and Tax Free Childcare.

        Questions people ask

        What is adjusted net income?

        It is your total taxable income minus certain deductions: gross personal pension contributions paid under relief at source, and Gift Aid donations. It is the figure HMRC uses for the £100,000 tests, the allowance taper, Tax Free Childcare, free hours and the Child Benefit charge. Salary sacrifice lowers your salary itself, so it lowers adjusted net income automatically.

        Is it really a 60% tax rate?

        It is an effective rate on the income band, not a published tax rate. The 40% higher rate applies to the pound itself, and withdrawing the allowance adds 20p for every pound because each £1 of income removes 50p of allowance that would otherwise have been tax free. Add 2% National Insurance and you get 62%, a figure that adds up to 71% with a Plan 2 student loan.

        Does the cliff apply to couples together?

        No, and that is part of what makes it so unfair. The Personal Allowance is tested person by person. Tax Free Childcare and 30 hours are lost if either parent is over £100,000. The Child Benefit charge is tested against the higher earner. So two parents each on £99,000 keep everything, while one parent on £101,000 and a partner who stays at home keeps less. Try the "Two earners on £99,000" example and then look at the couples insight in the full data.

        How can I bring my income below £100,000?

        The usual routes are pension contributions (workplace salary sacrifice, a net pay arrangement or a personal pension), Gift Aid donations, and salary sacrifice for approved benefits such as an electric car or a cycle to work scheme. You can also use unused pension allowance carried forward from the previous three tax years if you were in a registered pension scheme. Contributions must be made before 5 April. Bonuses can often be sacrificed into a pension before they are paid.

        Salary sacrifice, net pay or personal pension, what is the difference?

        With salary sacrifice you give up part of your salary in return for an employer pension contribution. It reduces your pay for income tax, National Insurance and student loan, so it is the most efficient route. With a net pay arrangement your employer deducts the pension from pay before income tax, but National Insurance and student loan are still charged on the full amount. With a personal pension (relief at source) you pay in 80% and the provider adds 20%. You then claim the extra higher rate relief, and the restored Personal Allowance, through your tax return or by asking HMRC to change your tax code. The explorer treats the last two as the same maths, since the final result is identical once relief has been claimed.

        Why can a pension payment increase my take home pay?

        Because several things switch back on when your adjusted net income falls to £100,000. You regain the Personal Allowance, recover Tax Free Childcare and funded hours, and for a parent that can be worth much more than the cash you put into the pension. Try the "Pension escape hatch" example. It is the reason many advisers talk about the £100,000 trap rather than the £100,000 rate.

        What is the pension annual allowance?

        For most people it is £60,000 a year, counting both your own and your employer's contributions. It tapers for those with adjusted income above £260,000, which is outside this explorer's range. Tax relief on your own personal contributions is limited to 100% of your earnings in the tax year. Unused allowance from the previous three years can be carried forward.

        What counts as money in your pocket?

        It is the cash left after tax, National Insurance, student loan and your own pension or Gift Aid payments, plus Child Benefit, minus the High Income Child Benefit Charge and childcare costs after any help. Pension money is saved, not spent, so the page also shows how much you add to your pension. Whether pension counts as "lost" is a personal choice, which is why both numbers are shown.

        What about the self employed, dividends and landlords?

        This explorer models employment income only. Dividends, interest and rental profits count towards adjusted net income and can drag you into the taper, and the self employed pay Class 4 National Insurance instead. The £100,000 rules apply to them in the same way, but the exact figures differ.

        Is this financial advice?

        No. It is an educational model with simplifying assumptions. Tax depends on your exact circumstances, tax codes, timing and how reliefs are claimed. Check figures against HMRC's own calculators or speak to a regulated adviser before making decisions, especially about pensions.
